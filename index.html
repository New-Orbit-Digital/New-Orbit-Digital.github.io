Evidence

One strategy instead of eleven small ones

Global Knowledge · Strategy

Global Knowledge’s marketing department was fractured across disciplines, and the internal team was drowning in one-off requests: run a small campaign here, a small campaign there. Each came with high creative lift, a budget too small to reach statistical confidence, no room to learn from the result, and an agency fee that would have exceeded the media spend behind it.

So the work wasn’t running those campaigns. It was reworking them into a single cohesive strategy — consolidating the asks, pointing the budget at what could actually be measured, and giving the internal team one plan to execute against instead of eleven competing ones. Reporting was standardized on GA4 as the single source of revenue truth, replacing the previous agency’s ads-attributed numbers.

$1,201,390 Revenue during the engagement

The revenue that couldn’t be attributed to anything

Codecademy & Skillsoft · GA4

Two separate enterprise accounts came to me with the same symptom: channel reports that made every source look broken. In both, the cause was the same and it wasn’t in the ad platform at all. Conversions were being sent server-side via Measurement Protocol, and the session parameters weren’t travelling with them.

Without ga_session_id, GA4 has no way to join a conversion back to the session that produced it. The revenue arrives; the attribution doesn’t. The fix was unglamorous — forward the session parameters from the client-side conversion moment into the server-side hit — but until it was done, every optimization decision downstream was being made on noise.

$334,665 In six-month revenue, now correctly attributed to source

Reading a funnel that ended in zero

DTC retail · Shopping

A store with steady Shopping traffic and no sales at all. The instinct in the room was to rebuild checkout. The event chain said otherwise:

8,897 users → 24 add to cart → 19 begin checkout → 8 add payment → 0 purchase

Cart to checkout held at 79% — that stage was fine. The collapse was at 0.27% product-page-to-cart, with a second anomaly at payment submission. I also ran the catalog against Merchant Center benchmarks: 44 products with comparable data, priced at parity on the top-clicked flagships and above benchmark on two others. Not a pricing crisis, but not a reason to spend more either.

My recommendation was to stop increasing spend. I’d also floated a theory about free-listing misattribution inflating the traffic; reconciling Shopping clicks against GA4’s session count disproved it, so I withdrew it.

$30,000 In monthly spend held back from a broken funnel
